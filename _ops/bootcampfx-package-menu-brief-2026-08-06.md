## The Big Idea (one line for the website)

**"Stop shopping. Start training. Three plans. One fits."**

The whole page sells one reframe: *you don't need to figure out what you want — we already did.* That's the anti-friction weapon. Porch Pumpkins stopped asking "what do you want?" and started printing money with a menu. Same move, same result.

## The Ladder (working names + real numbers)

| Tier | Name | Price | What's In It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | **Foundation** | $199/mo or $549/12-wk block | 3x/week group boot camp. The habit-builder. Nothing fancy — just the work. |
| Target | **Forge** | $299/mo or $849/12-wk block | Everything in Foundation + bi-weekly form checks + weekly accountability check-in + basic nutrition framework. **"This is what most people who get results buy."** |
| Premium | **Command** | $449/mo or $1,249/12-wk block | Everything in Forge + priority coaching access + weekly 1-on-1 touchpoint + advanced programming (recovery, strength, conditioning) + nutrition integration + monthly progress review. |
| Custom | **Private / Hybrid** | **Starts at $3,500** | One-off, multi-location, special-needs, or "I want it my way" clients. Hard floor. Standard packages are the default; custom is the exception that pays for the privilege. |

**Why these numbers:**
- The $199 entry price is your "get in the door" — matches what people expect from a boot camp, low enough to not scare the hesitant.
- The **$299 middle is the money seat** — a $100 jump from entry but the value jump (form checks + accountability + nutrition) feels like $200. That's the Heather move: make the middle the obvious choice, and 90% of buyers walk into it.
- Command at $449 isn't for everyone — it's the aspirational tier that makes Forge look reasonable and gives your serious clients a home. Retention lives here.
- The $3,500 custom floor is non-negotiable. Below that, the answer is always "pick a package." Your time is the product — don't sell it for scraps.

## The Add-On Architecture (Heather's secret weapon)

Entry tier stays lean — *but* you sell add-ons the way she sells design:

- **Form Fix** (initial movement assessment + first form pass) — **+$99** → *95% of Foundation clients take it, because nobody wants to do 12 weeks of bad squats.*
- **Fuel Plan** (nutrition setup, one-time) — **+$149**
- **Pickup** equivalent = **check-in frequency upgrade** — most Forge clients add it.

The trick: the entry tier is priced to be incomplete. The add-ons are priced to be painless. Heather watched 95% add design — because the entry package *felt* naked without it. You're not upselling. You're completing the sale.

## The Guiding Question (two options, max)

Heather asks: *"How pumpkin-crazy are you?"* → two options → done.

Your version:

> **"How serious are you?"**
> - **"I just want to move."** → Foundation
> - **"I want to look and feel different."** → Forge
> - *(Command is for the ones who already know they're serious.)*

Never present five options. Two, maybe three. Choice paralysis kills conversion; a menu with a default kills it dead.

## Payment Policy (Heather's zero-risk move)

1. **Everything paid upfront** — or locked installments (3x), no pause, no mid-block refunds. Heather took near-zero risk; you should too. Cash flow becomes predictable, and the client's commitment becomes real.
2. **Pay-in-full bonus:** 10% off the block or a free Form Fix. Makes upfront feel like a win, not a penalty.
3. **No "pause" button.** Pausing is how memberships die quietly. Locked blocks, defined start/end. If life happens, they can donate the remaining sessions — that's the policy, written in advance so you're never the bad guy.

## Website Structure (menu, not customizer)

Kill the "contact us for pricing" button. Kill the build-your-own form. Replace with:

1. **Headline:** "Three Plans. One Fits. No Guesswork."
2. **The question first:** "How serious are you?" (the two-option selector)
3. **Three cards** — name, price, what's inside, in outcome language:
   - Foundation: *"Move. Sweat. Build the habit."*
   - Forge: *"The plan most people who get results buy."*
   - Command: *"For the ones who want the full engine."*
4. **One CTA:** "Pick your plan" — never "learn more."
5. **The P.S. (Halbert rule — it's the second read):** "Every plan comes with a 30-day 'this isn't for me' guarantee. If you show up and hate it, we'll find you a better fit or refund the unused block. You can't lose. You can only quit."

Headline options if you want to A/B test:
- "Who Else Wants A Training Plan That Doesn't Require A Phone Call?"
- "Warning: Don't Email Us Asking 'How Much Is It?' Until You Read This"
- "Stop Shopping For Fitness. Start Choosing."

## The Strategic Layer (Gary's lens)

- **AIDA on the page:** Attention (headline) → Interest (the question) → Desire (outcome language per tier) → Action (one CTA).
- **Starving crowd:** your existing list of past clients and current members is your hottest list — launch the new menu to *them* first. Recency + frequency + unit-of-sale, all three, already proven.
- **Proof beats promise:** put numbers on the page — "20+ years, X clients, Y pounds lost" — before the prices. Heather's business sells itself because the outcome is *visible*. Your before/afters are your pumpkin carousel.
- **Productize the know-how next:** Heather coaches at $4,500 and franchises. Your version is the online/home program — same playbook, next year. The menu is step one; the productized program is step two.

## Expanded Implementation Notes (from follow-up review)

### Simple visual menu (restaurant-style)
- No long configurators.
- Each package shows: exact inclusions, duration, what is *not* included, price, and one-sentence outcome.
- Guided question on the page or in sales: "How serious are you about results this cycle?" → recommend two options max.
- Clear add-on list only where it does not break the package (e.g., end-of-cycle assessment, extra recovery sessions, private form video review).
- Upfront payment or locked installment language front-and-center.
- Social proof tied to each tier (retention stats, before/after, client quotes that match the package level).
- Mobile-first, fast load, one primary CTA per package.

### Operational rules to lock in
- No open quoting below the custom threshold.
- Track which package converts highest and which clients stay longest → feed back into the ladder.
- Price the middle package so the value gap to the top feels justified and the gap to the bottom feels like a real step down.
- Keep the menu short. Three core + one custom is enough.

### Confidence
- **High** on the packaging principle (proven in a high-margin local service business with the same friction problem).
- **Medium** on exact dollar amounts and names until we run the current BootCampFX numbers against the new structure.

### Immediate next actions
1. Pull current package mix, average ticket, and drop-off points in the sales process.
2. Draft the three packages with exact inclusions and prices.
3. Build the new packages page wireframe / copy.
4. Soft-launch with new leads only for 2–3 weeks, then roll fully.

## Bottom line

Lock the three-tier ladder, kill open-ended custom below $3,500, sell add-ons on the entry tier, demand upfront payment, and let the website be a menu with one question and one button. You'll raise average ticket, cut sales friction, and protect your coaching capacity — all at once.
